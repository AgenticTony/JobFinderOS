## Mon Sep 7 — ✍️ CV Fix #1 · "Your CV is 4 pages long. Is that actually a problem?"

---

"Your CV is 4 pages long."

I've seen this exact comment under a lot of CVs in job groups, including here.
It's usually true, but the reason matters more than the page count.

4 pages isn't a problem because of paper. It's a problem because:

- The person reading spends under 10 seconds on the first pass. Pages 3 and 4
  are functionally invisible.
- Long CVs usually mean every task you ever did is listed, instead of the
  achievements that matter for THIS job.
- It signals you can't prioritise — which is itself a work skill.

The fix isn't cutting your life story. It's this:

- Last 10 years: detailed, with achievements
- Anything older: job title, company, dates, one line. Or a single "Earlier
  experience" line
- Anything irrelevant to the job you want now: gone

2 pages works for almost everyone. 3 if you're senior or academic.

The exception: if a recruiter asks for your full history, send everything.
But the first document should be the short one.

How long is yours right now — and would you cut the jobs you're proud of?

## Tue Sep 8 — 🔎 Job Ad Breakdown #1 · "Fast-paced environment"

---

New series I want to try: taking phrases from real job ads and translating
them into normal English.

First one: "fast-paced environment".

What it can genuinely mean:
✅ There's real momentum, decisions happen quickly, you won't be bored

What it sometimes means:
⚠️ Understaffed, and the pace comes from doing two people's jobs
⚠️ Constant interruptions are normal, deep work isn't
⚠️ People burn out and get replaced

How to tell which one you're applying to:
- Ask in the interview: "What does a typical week look like in this role?" If
  the answer is a wall of "prioritising competing demands" — dig further.
- Ask why the role is open. Growth sounds very different from "replacing
  someone who left after 8 months".
- Check reviews on the employer, but weight the recent ones.

"Fast-paced" isn't automatically bad. Just don't read it as pure positivity.

What does it usually turn out to mean at your workplaces — the good version
or the other one?

## Wed Sep 9 — 🎤 Interview Q of the Week #1 · "Tell me about yourself"

---

The most common interview question is also the most wasted.

Most people answer it with a biography: born, raised, first job, every job
since. By minute three the interviewer has stopped listening.

Here's the thing: "tell me about yourself" doesn't mean "tell me your story".
It means "why are you in this chair, and why should I keep you in it?"

A structure that works — present, past, future:

- Present: "I'm a [role] at [company / currently job searching after X], where
  I focus on [main thing you do]"
- Past: "Before that I [the one experience most relevant to THIS job], where
  I [one concrete result]"
- Future: "Which is why this role interests me — [one honest, specific reason]"

90 seconds. Practise it out loud until it doesn't sound memorised.

The trap to avoid: starting with where you grew up. Nobody has ever been
hired for their hometown.

## Thu Sep 10 — 🧠 Transferable Skills #1 · Hospitality → office

---

Worked in hospitality and convinced you have no office experience?

You have more than people who've only worked in offices. You just have it
under different names.

Hospitality → office, translated:

- "Served customers during dinner rush" → working under pressure, prioritising
- "Dealt with an angry guest, everyone left happy" → conflict resolution,
  customer service
- "Handled the till and end-of-day count" → cash handling, accuracy, trust
- "Coordinated with kitchen and bar so orders landed together" → cross-team
  coordination, the actual definition of project work
- "Trained new starters" → onboarding, leadership

None of that is "just waitressing". That's a operations toolkit.

The trick on your CV: write the transferable skill in the bullet, not just
the task. "Coordinated service between kitchen, bar and floor for a 80-cover
restaurant" reads office-ready. "Waited tables" doesn't.

What's your background? I'll suggest what it translates to in the comments.

## Fri Sep 11 — 🚩 Red Flags #1 · "5 signs a job ad might be a scam"

---

Fake job ads get better every year, and job seekers are the target. Five
signs it might not be real:

1. The money moves the wrong way. A real employer pays you. Any "training
   fee", "equipment deposit", "visa processing cost" — walk away. Instantly.
2. You're hired without a conversation. Offer letter after a few messages?
   No interview at all? Real hiring involves actual humans meeting you.
3. Vague company, no company. No real website, no address, a generic Gmail
   for a "major international firm". The company exists — check it does.
4. Pay that makes no sense. 40k/month for basic data entry from home is not
   generosity. It's bait.
5. Urgency and secrecy. "Reply within 2 hours." "Don't tell anyone."
   Legitimate offers survive you thinking about them for a day.

The pattern behind all five: they move fast and they want something from you
early. Real employers are slower and want nothing but your time and skills.

Save this one. And if something feels off, drop the ad in the comments —
happy to take a look.

## Sat Sep 13 — 🇸🇪 Working in Sweden #1 · Provperiod

⚠️ **VERIFY before posting:** re-check current LAS rules (max 6 months,
notice during probation) at Arbetsförmedlingen or the law text itself. Only
post after this month's check. Swedish version needs a native-speaker check.

**English version (international-oriented SE groups):**

---

If you're job hunting in Sweden: most permanent contracts start with a
provperiod — a probation period of up to 6 months.

What it actually means (this surprises a lot of newcomers):

- During probation, notice is normally just 14 days — either side, if nothing
  else was agreed
- That cuts both ways: easier for you to leave, easier for them to end it
- It's not a red flag by itself. Most employers use it honestly, and most
  probations end in a confirmed job
- After probation, the ordinary notice periods apply and job security jumps
  considerably

Two things to check before you sign:
- Is the provperiod 6 months or shorter — never longer
- Does the contract say anything different about notice during probation?
  If yes, ask why

Your rights still exist during probation — probation is not "no rules",
it's "shorter notice".

Swedes and long-term residents: what's your experience — is 6 months
standard in your field, or do employers offer 3?

**Swedish version (Swedish-language groups — native check first):**

---

Jobbar du med jobbsök i Sverige? De flesta fasta anställningar börjar med
provperiod, upp till 6 månader.

Vad det egentligen betyder (mycket förvånar nya i Sverige):

- Under provperioden är uppsägningstiden oftast 14 dagar — för båda parter,
  om inget annat avtalats
- Det gäller båda håll: lättare för dig att sluta, lättare för dem att avsluta
- Det är ingen varningsflagga i sig. De flesta arbetsgivare använder provperioden
  som den är tänkt, och de flesta provperioder slutar med fast jobb
- Efter provperioden gäller de ordinarie uppsägningstiderna — och tryggheten
  ökar ordentligt

Två saker att kolla innan du skriver under:
- Står provperioden till 6 månader — aldrig längre
- Säger avtalet något annat om uppsägning under provperioden? Fråga varför

Dina rättigheter finns kvar under provperioden — det är "kortare uppsägningstid",
inte "inga regler".

Vad är standard inom ditt fält — 6 månader eller 3?
