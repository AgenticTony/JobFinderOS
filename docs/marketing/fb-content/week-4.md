## Mon Sep 28 — 🔎 Job Ad Breakdown #3 · "Willing to wear many hats"

The entertaining one. End the series' month on a laugh that's also useful.

---

Job ad phrase, translated: "willing to wear many hats".

What it sounds like: variety! Excitement! A role with range!

What it frequently means:

🎩 Hat 1: the job in the title
🎩 Hat 2: the job of the person who left and wasn't replaced
🎩 Hat 3: whatever nobody else wants to do
🎩 Hat 4: IT support, event planner, and social media manager, none of which
        are in your job description

"Wear many hats" is sometimes genuinely a startup-ish role where range is
the point and the pay matches. Sometimes it's one salary for three jobs,
and the hats keep arriving after you start.

How to tell before you accept:

- Ask: "Which hats come with the role today, and which might get added?"
  A hiring manager who's honest about it will say so.
- Ask how the team is structured and how long the current gaps have existed.
  "We're a small team, everyone chips in" + no plan to hire = hats forever.
- Salary test: does the pay look like one role or three? If it's one-role
  pay and the ad describes three roles, the maths is doing something.

None of this means don't apply. Many-hats roles are genuinely great early
in a career or when you WANT range. Just walk in knowing how many heads
you're being hired to be.

suspect the average here is four. 😄

## Tue Sep 29 — 🗂 Systems #1 · "If I was starting my job search tomorrow"

---

If I lost my job today and had to start again tomorrow, here's the exact
day I'd run. Not motivational-poster stuff — logistics.

**Morning (the work):**
- 08:00–09:00 — New jobs only: official job boards (in Sweden: Platsbanken;
  UK: the big boards + company career pages). Save anything plausible, don't
  apply yet
- 09:00–10:30 — Apply to the 2–3 strongest from yesterday's saves. Proper
  tailored applications, not the same letter pasted 40 times
- 10:30–11:00 — Follow-ups: anything older than 2 weeks without a reply gets
  one polite nudge. One. Not two.

**Midday (the leverage):**
- 11:00–11:30 — One human contact: a former colleague, a LinkedIn message
  about a role, a comment somewhere visible. Most jobs travel through people,
  not portals
- 11:30–12:00 — Skill gap work: the one thing that keeps appearing in ads
  you don't yet have. 30 focused minutes a day beats a course you never finish

**Afternoon (the admin):**
- 12:00–12:30 — Log everything in one place: role, date sent, contact,
  status. The searching fades from memory within days; the log is the only
  honest picture of what's working

**Then stop.**

The "then stop" is the important part. Job hunting 12 hours a day produces
burnout and 200 identical applications. A focused half-day, every day,
produces 15 good applications a week and the energy to interview well when
they land.

Quality beats volume. It's not a slogan — it's the only part of job-search
advice that holds up.
