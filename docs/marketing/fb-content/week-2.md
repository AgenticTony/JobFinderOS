## Mon Sep 14 — ✍️ CV Fix #2 · "What to remove from your CV immediately"

---

Your CV has one job: get you the interview. Everything that doesn't serve
that job is working against you.

Things to delete today:

1. The photo (in the UK and Sweden it's not expected — it invites bias you
   can't control. Some countries differ, but a photo is rarely helping you)
2. "References available on request" — everyone knows. It's dead space
3. Your full address — city or area is enough
4. Date of birth, age, marital status, nationality — not needed, and in the
   UK employers often remove this before shortlisting anyway
5. Skill bars / star ratings — "Excel ★★★★" means nothing. Either you can do
   it or you can't describe it properly
6. "Team player, results-driven, self-starter" — adjectives anyone can type.
   Replace each with one line showing you doing the thing
7. Hobbies that aren't relevant — keep them only if they're genuinely
   interesting or connect to the role
8. Every job before the last 10–15 years — summarise them in one line each
   or a single "earlier experience" block

None of this is hiding who you are. It's removing everything that dilutes
the parts that get you hired.

What's on yours that you've been unsure about? Ask below, I'll say keep or cut.

## Tue Sep 15 — 🔎 Job Ad Breakdown #2 / 💰 Salary Talk #1 · "Competitive salary"

---

Job ad phrase, translated: "competitive salary".

What it tells you: almost nothing. It's the salary equivalent of "results may
vary".

What it usually hides:

- A band so wide they don't want to anchor themselves
- A number below market, hoping the rest of the ad sells it
- Occasionally: genuinely good pay, with room to negotiate

What it does NOT mean: "we'll beat your current salary". Don't assume.

What to do with it:

- Sweden: many roles sit under a kollektivavtal (collective agreement) which
  sets the actual range. Asking "is the role covered by a kollektivavtal?"
  is a normal, professional question that instantly narrows things down.
- UK: job boards increasingly force bands. If the band is missing or absurdly
  wide ("£25,000–£45,000"), the real number is usually near the bottom.
- Either country: ask the range in the FIRST call. It's expected. "Could you
  share the budgeted range so we don't waste each other's time?" — recruiters
  respect that question far more than candidates expect.

"Competitive" is decided by comparing offers. Your job is to make sure
there ARE offers to compare.

Has "competitive salary" ever turned out to actually be competitive for you?

## Wed Sep 16 — 🎤 Interview Q of the Week #2 · "What's your biggest weakness?"

---

Everyone's least favourite question. Here's how to survive it without the two
classic disasters:

Disaster 1: the humble-brag. "I'm a perfectionist." "I work too hard."
Interviewers hear this weekly. It reads as either dishonest or
self-unaware. You've answered nothing.

Disaster 2: actual disqualification. "I'm always late." "I don't take
feedback well." Now they have a real reason to reject you.

What actually works — a real weakness + proof you're handling it:

1. Pick something true, minor, and fixable
2. Show the fix you already applied
3. Show it's working

Example: "I used to say yes to everything and then juggle too much. Now I
force myself to write down my current commitments before taking on anything
new. It's made me much more reliable with deadlines."

Or: "Public speaking didn't come naturally to me. I started volunteering to
present in team meetings — small rooms first. Last month I ran a session for
20 people."

The question isn't hunting for your flaw. It's checking whether you know
yourself and do something about it. That's all.

What's your answer been so far — honest one or the perfectionist one? 😄

## Thu Sep 17 — 🧠 Transferable Skills #2 · Warehouse → logistics, operations, procurement

---

Warehouse work on your CV and you're applying for "office" jobs? You're
closer than you think.

Warehouse → translated:

- "Picked and packed to daily targets" → performance against KPIs, working
  under time pressure
- "Managed stock locations and cycle counts" → inventory control, data
  accuracy
- "Booked in deliveries, matched to purchase orders" → goods-in, supplier
  follow-up — that's procurement support
- "Planned load order so the last drop comes off first" → route logic,
  planning — the literal core of logistics
- "Trained new agency staff on safety and process" → instruction,
  responsibility, compliance awareness
- "Flagged stock discrepancies and traced them back" → root-cause
  investigation, reporting

Roles where this lands directly: logistics coordinator, inventory planner,
operations assistant, procurement/purchasing admin, supply chain support.

Same trick as always: on the CV, write the skill, not just the task.
"Managed cycle counts with 99%+ accuracy" beats "did stock counts".

tasks in the comments.
