## 1. The category's weak point is BILLING, not features

Every competitor sells volume and most of them have clean-ish product
reviews. What they get one-starred for is how they charge.

## 2. The market has turned against volume

2026 data on AI-written applications:

- **49% of hiring managers auto-dismiss AI resumes**
- **65%** say AI-enhanced resumes made it harder to verify claimed skills
- **86%** are worried about inflated claims
- **67%** of HR leaders say AI applications slowed their hiring
- ~40% of applications show clear signs of AI generation

The canonical example in that reporting: an AI-generated resume citing a
**PMP certification the candidate had never pursued.**

> **CAVEAT — read before quoting any of these.** Most come from resume-writing
> services and career coaches, who have an obvious interest in "AI resumes
> fail". The [Forbes piece](https://www.forbes.com/sites/rachelwells/2026/03/18/ai-resumes-are-sabotaging-the-hiring-process-67-of-managers-reveal/)
> is the most independent source. Treat the DIRECTION as solid and the exact
> percentages as marketing. If you publish a number, publish it with its
> source and date, and prefer the Forbes one.

## 3. What is SAFE to publish — and what isn't

**Do not name competitors with their scores.** Three reasons:

1. **It ages badly.** A page saying "LazyApply: 2.1" is false the week it
   moves, and you will not notice.
2. **EU comparative advertising rules** (Directive 2006/114/EC) require
   comparisons to be objective, verifiable and non-denigrating. Quoting a
   rival's BBB rating is closer to denigration than comparison. Worth one
   question to the legal hour already scheduled before first paying user.
3. **It reads defensive.** A new product attacking incumbents by name looks
   smaller than one simply stating what it does.

**Do invert the complaints into positive promises.** Every one below is a
competitor's worst review turned into a commitment — unfalsifiable, ageless,
and it needs no comparison to land.

## 4. The copy that comes out of this

### The billing block (WO-16's five promises, verbatim)

> **No credits. No surprise charges.**
> The trial doesn't auto-renew. Cancel in one click.
> And if you get hired, we refund every month you haven't used.

Each clause maps to a specific complaint: credits→AIApply, surprise
charges→AIApply, auto-renew→Sonara, one-click cancel→Jobright,
refund→LazyApply. Nobody named, every claim ours to keep.

### The differentiator (the strongest line we have)

> **Every claim in your tailored CV is checked against your real CV
> before it's sent.**

No competitor advertises any verification of AI output against the source.
This maps directly onto the #1 recruiter complaint in §2, and we can back it
with a measured rate — see WO-02. **Do not publish a fabrication-rate number
until the re-measurement runs through the corrected checker.**

### The anti-volume position

> We tell you when a job isn't worth applying for.

The category's business model depends on you applying constantly; ours
doesn't. Pair with the 49% auto-dismissal stat *if* you cite Forbes.

### The regional line

> Swedish jobs from Platsbanken, the official source. UK jobs from Reed.
> Your data stays in the EU.

Nobody international does Swedish public job data properly.

## 5. What we must NOT claim yet

Honesty is the whole positioning; the landing page cannot be the one place
we stretch.

- ❌ A fabrication-rate percentage — stale until WO-02 re-measures
- ❌ Testimonials, user counts, "trusted by" — there are none
- ❌ "Never lies" / "guaranteed accurate" — the guard flags and blocks, it
  does not certify. Say what it does: checks, regenerates, blocks, shows you
  the claim
- ❌ Job-count or match-quality figures — the pool is small and unmeasured
- ❌ Any competitor's name, score, or price
